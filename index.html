Drought from Space

Vegetation health across the American West: where did the land brown during two decades of drought, and how fast did it green up again?

Growing-season greenness vs normal, 2021

Each square is ~25 km. Brown = less green than its 2001–2020 average; teal = greener. Hover for details, click a state to focus it.

States, ranked

Average change vs normal, 2021. Click a bar to focus a state.

Monthly greenness vs normal: whole West

12-month rolling average of the monthly anomaly. Drag across the chart to average the map over several years; click once to jump to a year. Shaded bands mark major drought periods.

Data & transformations. MODIS/Terra monthly NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index, 0 = bare ground, ~0.9 = dense forest) from NASA GIBS, 2001–2025. Pixel colors were converted back to NDVI using the layer's published colormap (values are binned to the colormap's step size). Map: April–September monthly values averaged per year, then averaged into 0.25° cells; cells assigned to the state covering most of their area. A few cells left empty by seams in the source imagery are filled with the average of their neighbors; cells that still have no data are hatched. “Change vs normal” subtracts each cell's own 2001–2020 growing-season average. Timeline: state-wide mean NDVI per month minus that state's 2001–2020 mean for the same calendar month, smoothed with a 12-month trailing average.
